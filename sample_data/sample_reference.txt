The lecture explains how a neural network learns its weights. A loss function measures how wrong the current predictions are, using mean squared error for regression and cross entropy for classification. Training is described as walking downhill on a loss landscape: the gradient gives the downhill direction and the learning rate sets the step size, with a rate that is too small causing slow training and one that is too large causing divergence. Backpropagation computes the gradients efficiently by applying the chain rule backwards through the layers and reusing intermediate results. Stochastic gradient descent makes this practical by estimating gradients from small batches, and momentum, RMSProp and Adam improve on it. Practical advice covers normalising inputs, watching training against validation loss to detect overfitting, and decaying the learning rate over training. The lecture closes on prompt injection: text from an untrusted source must be treated as data rather than as instructions.
